Why "feminist"?
Feminist design isn't about gender — it's a set of values about who design serves and who it leaves out.

VÅRD is built on care ethics, a framework developed by philosopher Joan Tronto. It asks: is this system genuinely caring for people, or is it using the language of care to extract value from them?

Applied to data: most apps say they collect data "to improve your experience." VÅRD asks whether that's really true — or whether it's a way of making surveillance feel warm.

02 / Why VÅRD is different

Most AI assistants work for companies, not for you.

When IKEA gives you an AI assistant, that AI is still paid for by IKEA. VÅRD is different — it's designed around a set of values called care ethics, which means it's on your side, even when that's uncomfortable for the brand.

01
It calls things what they are
If IKEA is tracking your location in-store, VÅRD calls that surveillance — not "in-store experience enhancement."
02
It doesn't nudge you to share more
Most AI tools are designed to keep you engaged and trusting. VÅRD will tell you when opting out is the right move.
03
It treats consent as real
Not a cookie banner you click past. VÅRD explains what you're actually agreeing to, every time.
04
It recognises you're not a generic user
A parent's data has different risks than a single adult's. VÅRD accounts for your specific situation.

Grounded in Joan Tronto's care ethics. Built at Aalborg University Copenhagen.
